1 · The Balanced theme

Your read on the two themes is right, and it is a value problem, not a colour problem. Dark sits on #171411 with near-black inputs — rich, but heavy; every screen is midnight. Light sits on ivory with white cards — airy, but the warmth drains out; it can read generic. Balanced keeps the exact same seven brand colours and typography and moves only the ground: a warm espresso ladder, roughly two stops lighter than Dark.

What Balanced changes, in words:

  • Ground and cards lift from near-black to warm espresso, on a deliberately wide value ladder — ground #332D26 → cards #443C32 → inputs #60564A — so each surface separates on its own, without leaning on hairline borders. (You spotted this \u2014 and Dark's ladder has now been widened the same way: cards #322B23, inputs #453D33.)
  • #C39F77 comes home. Heritage Sand becomes the working accent — primary CTAs, links, focus rings, the rule-line moments — with ink text on top (strong contrast). Safari Brass #A8794B steps down to hover/pressed and deep accents, so the pair reads as one metal catching different light.
  • Borders gain two steps of visibility and placeholder text lightens, fixing the low-separation issues we patched one by one on Dark.

It is delivered as tokens/theme-balanced.css — additive, nothing existing touched — and activates with data-theme="balanced" on any root, exactly like Light. See it live in Theme Compare. If you approve it, the decision to make is which theme is the default for each product; my suggestion is Balanced for the flagship site and both editions kept for bookings (travellers toggle).

Complementary palettes worth considering

The system is intentionally two-metal. If you want more range without breaking it, these three families sit naturally under the brand — used one per screen at most, never as CTAs:

Mopane green — conservation stories, positive-impact badges, success states.
Kalahari clay — special offers, editorial pull-quotes, seasonal campaigns.
Riverine slate — maps, practical/logistics UI, insurance and documents.

2 · What the references do — and what we do better

Safari.com — the closest model for tone

Quiet, editorial, low density: one idea per band, serif headlines with a single italicised word, enormous real photography (a lodge page carries an 18-photo gallery). The craft is in the reassurance layer: a reply-time promise ("as little as 15 minutes") runs from the top bar to every form footer; microcopy under each field explains why it is asked ("Used only for trip coordination. No sales calls."); pricing is disarmed head-on ("You pay the lodge's rate, never a markup").

Their lodge page is the anatomy lesson: sticky in-page anchors (Overview · Availability · Rates · Offers · Family · Gallery), a live availability check, a full seasonal rate table with currency switcher and validity dates, family policy in specific detail, current offers in prose, and a named specialist with photo attached to the lodge, backed by one dated traveller quote. Their team page gives every specialist a photo, WhatsApp and email, and two dated verbatim reviews; the enquiry wizard anchors budget in real numbers ($1,000 / $1,500 / $2,500+ pp per day) and lets guests pick contact channel, WhatsApp and iMessage included.

Go2Africa — the planning IA

Warmer and denser. The trust arsenal is industrial: review platform score and count with recency stamps, award logos, B-Corp status. Language is "Help me plan", not "Enquire". Destinations split into why-go / where-to-go / when-to-go; there is a travel-by-month entry point; tour cards carry from–to price ranges so budget expectations are set on the card, not after a call; itineraries carry positive-impact tags.

Rhino Africa — the authority voice

Bold claims stated flatly (the authority framing, traveller counts, most-awarded), a video hero, and partner-brand logo walls — the lodges they sell (Singita-tier names) doing the proving for them. Density sits between the other two.

What we already do better than all three

  • One design language end to end. All three references break style the moment you leave marketing — funnels drop into plain forms. Our marketing site and booking flow are one system, and that seamlessness is the brand promise.
  • A distinctive ground. All three sit on white. Our dark-luxury identity (and Balanced keeps this) is ownable shelf-space none of them occupies.
  • A real self-serve booking flow with transparent fee lines and live-rate framing — the references all funnel to "talk to us".
  • Typographic restraint and a token-driven system — three switchable themes from one component set; none of them can do this.

3 · Product assessments

Flagship website

Strong
  • Cinematic dark heroes with the mandated scrim; photography-first layouts ready for real imagery.
  • Editorial band rhythm (ivory / sand / white) gives long pages pace.
  • Lora + Poppins pairing is distinctive and consistently applied.
  • Enquire form is clean and honest — no dark patterns.
Weak
  • Navigation is four flat links; every reference runs a deep destination/experience menu. Our IA is the thinnest part of the product.
  • Proof layer is nearly absent: no named, dated guest quotes; no award / membership strip; no review score anywhere.
  • Enquire asks for details but gives nothing back: no reply-time promise, no budget anchors, no contact-channel choice, no reassurance microcopy.
  • Footer is thin — references use footers as a second homepage (featured trips, reads, contact channels).
  • No visible human: no phone, no WhatsApp, no specialist faces.
Missing entirely — destination & experience library (the core content type of this industry); lodge detail page with rates/availability; team page; testimonials page; travel-by-month / seasonality tool; brochures or any lead magnet; search.

Direct bookings

Strong
  • Complete funnel — results → property → checkout → confirmation — with a stepper that keeps guests oriented.
  • Filters are genuinely useful and now work as a mobile sheet.
  • Fee transparency (conservation levy as a line item) builds trust the references don't attempt in-flow.
  • Light/dark parity held across every screen.
Weak
  • Result cards are interchangeable — no "known for" one-liner, no differentiator between a R12k and R45k lodge beyond price.
  • Property page is thin against the Safari.com anatomy: no seasonal rate table, no what's-included, no family policy, no current offers, no anchor nav.
  • Checkout has no reassurance near payment (secure-payment cue, cancellation terms, "a human checks every booking").
  • Confirmation dead-ends — no onward path.
Missing entirely — compare tray (2–3 lodges side by side); map view of results; saved / wishlist; a named specialist escape hatch ("prefer a human? Thandi knows this reserve"); post-booking trip hub (documents, transfers, packing, one contact).

Component library & templates

Strong
  • 27+ components with real states, tokens, icon set, and now the mobile drawer — coverage for what's built is solid.
  • Three deck templates keep internal material on-brand.
Gaps the roadmap above will need
  • RateTable (seasonal rates), AnchorNav/Tabs, Breadcrumb, ReviewCard (quote + name + date + source), SpecialistCard, StatStrip, Itinerary Timeline (day-by-day), Gallery/lightbox, MapSlot, Toast.
  • Templates: itinerary/proposal document (already agreed as priority 2), guest email set, destination one-pager.

4 · Suggestions, ranked by impact

  1. Build the proof layer everywhere.Medium
    Named + dated guest quotes, review score + count, award/membership strip, partner-lodge logo wall. This is the single widest gap between us and all three references, and it costs no new pages — it threads into existing ones.
  2. Deepen the lodge/property page to the full anatomy.Large
    Anchor nav, seasonal rate table with validity dates + currency, what's-included, family policy, current offers, gallery. This page is where safari decisions actually happen; ours is currently a summary, the references' are a dossier.
  3. Put humans in the product.Medium
    SpecialistCard attached to lodges, enquiry and checkout; a team page template with per-person reviews and WhatsApp/email. "Orchestrated by people who live this" is your differentiator — currently invisible.
  4. Upgrade the enquire flow into a planning conversation.Medium
    Reply-time promise, budget tiers with real per-day anchors, contact-channel choice (WhatsApp included), reassurance microcopy under fields, an example-filled trip-details prompt.
  5. Give the flagship a real IA.Large
    Destinations and Experiences as first-class sections with a mega-menu; why-go / when-to-go / where-to-stay structure per destination; travel-by-month entry point.
  6. Adopt Balanced (pending your eye on the compare page)Small
    and decide per-product defaults. The token file is done; adoption is a one-line attribute per product.
  7. Commission the imagery program.Medium
    Every layout is photography-first and every slot is a placeholder. 6–10 hero-grade lodge/wildlife photos would change perceived quality more than any code.
  8. Results differentiation: a "known for" line per lodge, compare tray, map toggle.Medium
  9. The itinerary/proposal artifact (agreed priority 2) — day-by-day timeline, printable, specialist-signed.Medium
  10. Post-booking trip hub.Large
    Confirmation currently dead-ends; the "seamless in between" story lives here — documents, transfers, packing, one named contact.
  11. Checkout reassurance strip — secure payment, cancellation summary, human-checked bookings.Small
  12. Footer as second homepage + visible contact channels sitewide.Small
