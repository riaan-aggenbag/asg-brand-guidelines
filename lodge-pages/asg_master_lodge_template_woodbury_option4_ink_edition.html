Overview

Stone and thatch above the river valley

Woodbury Lodge stands against a cliff above the Bushman’s River valley — seven stone-and-thatch chalets tucked among indigenous trees alive with birdsong, connected by stone walkways, each with a private deck and panoramic views over the plains. Family-run since 1999, the lodge keeps its welcome personal: high tea with homemade breads and cakes, drinks by the pool overlooking the plains, storytelling around the campfire under the stars.

Meals are all-inclusive, two guided game drives a day come with every stay, and elephants are regular late-afternoon visitors below the lodge. The en-suite bathrooms carry a small luxury you notice on winter mornings: heated floors. Two family chalets with inter-leading twin rooms welcome children from six years.

Game viewing is rewarding year-round and malaria-free. Dry winter is peak for sightings; green summer brings lush scenery and newborn animals.

Questions

Frequently asked

Are game drives and meals included?

Yes — meals are all-inclusive and two guided game drives a day come with every stay. High tea with homemade breads and cakes is a Woodbury tradition.

What are the chalets like?

Seven stand-alone stone-and-thatch chalets among indigenous trees, each with a private deck over the plains, air conditioning, minibar, and an en-suite bathroom with heated floors.

Are children welcome?

Children from 6 years are welcome. The two family chalets sleep up to four, with an inter-leading twin room for privacy. Guests 12 and over are charged as adults.

Is Woodbury Lodge malaria-free?

Yes — like the rest of Amakhala, Woodbury Lodge is malaria-free. No antimalarial medication is needed at any time of year.

Will we see elephants?

Very likely — the resident herd regularly passes below the lodge in the late afternoon, and elephant sightings on Amakhala are classed as common.

Will we see lions?

Lions are resident and actively tracked. Like all wild sightings they’re never guaranteed — though with elephants passing below the lodge, the Big Five rarely feel far away.

Is there a spa or hot tub?

No spa and no hot tubs — Woodbury’s comforts run to heated bathroom floors, two pools overlooking the plains, and a fire under the stars.

Is there WiFi?

Free WiFi is available in the main lodge areas; the chalets themselves stay unplugged.

When is the best time to visit?

There’s no wrong month. Dry winter (May–September) gives the clearest sightings from the valley; green summer (October–March) brings calves, cubs and migratory birdlife.

What are the check-in and check-out times?

Arrive between 13:00 and 14:00; rooms are released by 11:00 on departure day. Ask your consultant about early or late arrangements — the lodge accommodates where it can.

How do we get there from Gqeberha (Port Elizabeth)?

Chief Dawid Stuurman International Airport is about 75 km by road — roughly an hour via the N2. Transfers can be arranged.

Can we visit Addo Elephant National Park?

Yes — Addo Elephant National Park is about 50 km from the lodge; your consultant can build a day visit into your itinerary.

Is there parking at the lodge?

Yes — free private parking on site, with accessible bays. No reservation is needed for the parking itself.

Can we bring pets?

No — pets are not allowed at Woodbury Lodge, in common with the rest of the reserve.

Can the restaurant cater for dietary requirements?

Yes — meals are taken at the lodge restaurant and are all-inclusive. Vegetarian, gluten-free and dairy-free options are available, and special diet menus can be arranged on request; tell your consultant when you book.

How much does a stay cost?

Rates start from $405 per person per night sharing, all-inclusive with two game drives a day. Rates vary by season and chalet type; your consultant confirms the exact rate for your dates in writing, with no booking fees.
